title: Phone-free school implementation lessons
description: Practical implementation lessons for Singapore schools based on programme-aligned rollout experience with the SG School Phone Pouch Programme.

---

The SG School Phone Pouch Programme is built around a consistent, visible routine:

- Each student places their phone into a **personal phone pouch** when entering a phone-free space.
- The pouch uses a **child-safe locking clip mechanism** and **does not use needles**.
- The pouch can be unlocked only using a **magnetic unlocking base**.
- The pouch **stays with the student throughout the day**, making compliance easy to show and audit.
- **Administrative staff can unlock pouches during emergencies** using an unlocking base.
- The pouches are **compatible with all phone models**.

These design choices support fast visual compliance checks and reduce reliance on bags/pockets where discreet phone use is still possible.

---

### Start small, then scale
- Begin with a structured trial for a single level or cohort to validate routines and reduce rollout friction.
- Use the trial period to refine unlocking base placement, staff scripts, and student movement flows.
- Scale only after routines feel automatic and consistently enforced.

### Make the routine highly visible
- Reinforce entry behaviour at the classroom door: phone into pouch, clip closed, pouch remains locked.
- Use simple signage and repeated language so expectations do not vary by teacher or venue.
- Keep the policy message consistent:
  **“In class, the phone is in a locked pouch.”**

### Stock readiness prevents ad hoc exceptions
- Maintain buffer stock of spare pouches for damaged or missing units so routines do not break.
- Keep replacement processes simple and centralised (e.g. via general office or designated admin point), with clear directions for staff.

### Plan unlocking points to support crowd flow
- Unlocking occurs only at **designated unlocking bases**, so location planning matters.
- For high-stakes or high-density spaces:
  - Place bases at exit points to distribute flow and maintain boundaries.
  - For examinations, students keep locked pouches in their bags during the paper and unlock only after the paper at approved points.

---

## Outcomes to measure (recommended, low-burden indicators)

Schools that want to evaluate impact should track operational indicators rather than relying on anecdotal impressions. Examples include:

- Teacher-reported frequency of phone-related lesson interruptions (before vs after)
- Start-of-lesson time lost to device management (before vs after)
- Volume of replacements issued (damage/loss) and time-to-replace
- Dismissal flow observations at unlocking bases (e.g. whether congestion occurs and whether base placement needs adjustment)
- Parent understanding/confidence based on clarity of communications and demonstrations of the safe, needle-free locking clip

These measures help schools evidence improvements in classroom focus and reduce staff workload without over-claiming academic outcomes.
