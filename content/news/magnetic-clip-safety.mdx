The SG School Phone Pouch Programme specifies that the phone pouch uses a **child-safe locking clip mechanism** and explicitly states that it **does not use needles**. This is positioned as a student-safety improvement compared with some overseas lockable pouch solutions that use needle-based mechanisms.

Key confirmed points:
- **No needles** are used in the locking mechanism.
- The locking design is intended to be **child-safe**.
- The pouch is unlocked using a **magnetic unlocking base** (i.e., access is controlled via an unlocking base rather than ad hoc opening).

---

## What schools should communicate

When explaining safety and handling to students and parents, schools can emphasise:

- The pouch is designed to secure phones in a way that supports **safe student handling**.
- The locking clip avoids **sharp pins/needle-based mechanisms**.
- Unlocking is controlled through **unlocking bases** at school-approved locations and times, supporting consistent routines and reducing negotiation.

This supports the programme’s operational objective: visible, easy-to-audit compliance in phone-free spaces.

---

## Operational implications for rollout

Because the standard programme workflow is:
- Phone into pouch on entry to phone-free space
- Pouch remains locked during lessons
- Unlocking occurs at designated locations using unlocking bases

Schools can plan enforcement and duty staffing around fixed unlocking points rather than collecting phones or running classroom storage.
