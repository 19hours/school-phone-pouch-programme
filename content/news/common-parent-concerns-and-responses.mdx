## What Parents Commonly Ask (and How to Respond)

This guidance helps schools respond consistently to caregiver concerns about the **SG School Phone Pouch Programme**. The programme is designed to reduce distraction in learning spaces through a routine that is **simple, visible, and easy to audit**:

- Students place their phones into a **personal phone pouch** when entering phone-free spaces.
- The pouch is secured using a **child-safe locking clip** (no needles or sharp pins).
- Phones are unlocked only at **school-designated locations** using a **magnetic unlocking base**.
- The pouch **stays with the student throughout the day**, so phones are present but not usable during lessons.
- In emergencies, **administrative staff can unlock pouches** using an unlocking base.

Core message to repeat:
**“In class, the phone is in a locked pouch.”**

---

## Key Responses to Share With Parents

### 1) “How will I reach my child in an emergency?”
- Parents should continue to contact the school through the **general office**, as per normal Singapore school practice.
- Phones remain with students (not confiscated), but are locked during lessons.
- In urgent situations, **administrative staff can unlock a pouch** using an unlocking base.

Recommended reassurance:
> “Your child’s phone is with them, but locked during lessons. If there is an emergency, the school can facilitate access quickly.”

---

### 2) “Is the locking mechanism safe?”
Yes. The programme design prioritises student safety:
- The pouch uses a **child-safe locking clip**.
- There are **no needles, sharp pins, or tools** involved.
- Unlocking is done using a **magnetic unlocking base** at approved locations.

This directly addresses common concerns about other solutions that use pin-based locking mechanisms.

---

### 3) “How do phones get returned each day?”
Phones are not collected centrally. Instead:
- The pouch remains with the student throughout the day.
- Phones are unlocked at approved times (e.g. dismissal) at **designated unlocking bases**.
- This routine is consistent across classrooms, assemblies, CCAs, camps, and exams.

This reduces disputes and loss risk compared with shared storage models.

---

### 4) “Can students unlock the pouch themselves?”
Students unlock only at approved locations using an unlocking base. The programme is designed so that:
- The pouch’s lock can be opened only with an unlocking base.
- Unlocking bases are placed at school-approved points to define **where and when** phone use is allowed.

(Each school should decide how bases are supervised operationally, based on site layout and student flow.)

---

### 5) “Will there be long queues at dismissal?”
Queue management is handled primarily through site planning:
- Schools can install multiple unlocking bases where needed to distribute flow.
- The programme includes **1 free unlocking base for every 100 pouches purchased**, with additional bases available at **$80 each**.
- If bottlenecks appear, schools can adjust base placement rather than changing the routine.

---

### 6) “How do staff quickly identify the right pouch or spot problems?”
Schools may use optional customisation to improve identification and reduce confusion:
- **Colour coding** by level/cohort (useful for quick visual recognition)
  - Colour selection is **free for quantities above 1000**
  - Available colours: Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Blue, Indigo, Purple, Black, Grey
- **Name card holder** (+ **$0.50 per pouch**) to help students identify ownership
- **School logo printing** (single-colour) (+ **$0.50 per pouch**, minimum **1000**)

These features help teachers and administrators spot issues quickly, especially in large assemblies or shared spaces.

## Suggested Communication Channels

- Include a one-page FAQ in:
  - Student handbook
  - School website / parent portal
- Use consistent wording across all communications:
  **“In class, the phone is in a locked pouch.”**
- Consider sharing a short demonstration (e.g. during parent engagement sessions) showing:
  - How the pouch is locked
  - How it is unlocked using an unlocking base
  - Safety reassurance (no needles or sharp pins)
- During the first weeks of rollout, provide a clear enquiry channel (e.g. general office) so parents know where to direct questions.
