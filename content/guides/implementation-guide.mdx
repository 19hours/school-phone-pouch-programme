## Programme Summary (What You Are Implementing)

The SG School Phone Pouch Programme is a schoolwide routine that secures student phones in **personal lockable pouches** when students enter **phone-free spaces** (e.g. classrooms, halls, exam venues).

Key operational features:
- Each student places their phone into a **personal pouch** at entry to a phone-free space.
- The pouch uses a **child-safe locking clip mechanism** (no needles or sharp pins).
- Pouches can be opened only using a **magnetic unlocking base**.
- The pouch **stays with the student throughout the day**, enabling quick visual compliance checks.
- In emergencies, pouches can be unlocked by **administrative staff** using an unlocking base.
- Teachers and administrators benefit from **fast, visible compliance** rather than relying on bags/pockets or claims like “it’s in my locker”.

---

## 1) Prepare Your Stakeholders

### Brief Teachers and School Leaders
Cover the rationale and the daily routine:
- Phones create distraction in learning spaces.
- Bags and pockets still allow discreet use.
- The pouch solution provides a consistent and easy-to-audit approach.

Operational emphasis for staff:
- Students keep their pouch with them all day; staff do not need to collect devices.
- Compliance checking is visual: students have a locked pouch, and it is easy to see.

### Brief Administrative Staff
Administrative staff should be aware of:
- How to support unlocking at designated points
- Emergency unlocking procedures (unlocking bases enable admin unlocking when required)

---

## 2) Define Phone-Free Spaces and Allowed Phone-Use Zones

Decide where phones must be secured, such as:
- Classrooms
- Assembly halls
- Examination venues
- Other school activities where phone-free time is required (e.g. CCAs, camps)

Decide where phones may be accessed:
- Only at locations with **unlocking bases**
- Only at school-approved times (e.g. dismissal or designated breaks)

Use a simple policy message that all staff can repeat consistently:
**“In class, the phone is in a locked pouch.”**

---

## 3) Plan Your Daily Workflow (Do Not Use Collection Baskets)

The programme is designed so that phones remain with the student but are unusable in phone-free spaces. Schools should **not** implement classroom baskets or centralised collection as the primary workflow.

### Entry to Phone-Free Spaces (Lock-In)
- Students place their phones into their **assigned personal pouch** when entering a phone-free space.
- Students lock the pouch using the built-in locking clip.
- Teachers perform quick visual checks without handling devices.

### During Lessons / Phone-Free Periods (Carry Locked Pouch)
- The locked pouch remains with the student throughout the day.
- Phones cannot be used discreetly in pockets or bags because the pouch provides visible compliance.

### When Phone Use Is Allowed (Unlock at Base)
- Students unlock pouches only at **designated unlocking bases**.
- Unlocking bases define where and when phones can be used.

---

## 4) Site Planning for Unlocking Bases

Unlocking bases are required for phone access:
- **1 free unlocking base is included for every 100 pouches purchased**
- **Additional unlocking bases are $80 each**

Schools should decide unlocking base placement to manage flow and enforce boundaries between phone-free zones and phone-use zones (e.g. outside classrooms, near exits, or other approved points). The objective is smooth movement and minimal bottlenecks.

## 6) Daily Checks, Replacements, and Operational Continuity

Daily compliance is primarily visual:
- Teachers verify that students have their pouch and that it is locked in phone-free spaces.

Replacement planning:
- Maintain a small buffer of spare pouches for damage, loss, and top-ups for new cohorts.
- Establish a straightforward replacement route (e.g. via general office or designated admin point), keeping the routine consistent across the school.

Emergency handling:
- Ensure staff know that pouches can be unlocked by administrative staff during emergencies using an unlocking base.

---

## 7) Communicate Clearly With Parents

Parent communication should be simple and aligned with how the programme operates in school:
- Phones remain with students, but are secured and not used during lessons.
- The pouch provides visible compliance and reduces distraction.
- Unlocking occurs only at approved locations using unlocking bases.

Recommended framing:
- A single sentence policy message parents can remember:
  **“In class, the phone is in a locked pouch.”**
- Where and when students may unlock their pouches (school-designated locations and times)
- How the school handles emergencies (admin unlocking when required)

Avoid introducing controls not used in this programme (e.g. “safety seals”, “unique numbering”, or “collection baskets”) unless the school has separately implemented them outside of the SG School Phone Pouch Programme.
