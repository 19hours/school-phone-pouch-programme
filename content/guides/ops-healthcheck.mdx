## Purpose of an Operations Health Check

Once the SG School Phone Pouch Programme is rolled out, ongoing success depends on **routine discipline, predictable operations, and early detection of friction points**.

A monthly health check allows schools to:
- Confirm that daily routines are being followed consistently
- Ensure sufficient buffer stock and operational readiness
- Identify emerging issues before they escalate into disputes or non-compliance

The emphasis is on **visible compliance and simplicity**, not punitive enforcement.

---

## Monthly Audit Focus Areas

### 1) Classroom and Cohort Spot Checks

Conduct sample checks across a portion of form classes each month to confirm:

- Students are carrying their **personal phone pouch** in phone-free spaces
- Pouches are:
  - Intact and serviceable
  - Locking clips are firm and functional
  - Free from damage caused by misuse or storage pressure
- Compliance is visually obvious (no reliance on “it’s in my bag” claims)

Checks should be observational and quick, aligned with the programme’s design.

---

### 2) Buffer Stock and Replacement Readiness

Confirm that the school maintains:
- A **central buffer of spare pouches** (e.g. held at the general office or designated admin point)
- Clear procedures for issuing replacements for:
  - Damaged pouches
  - Lost pouches
  - New intakes or mid-year transfers

Because pouches stay with students throughout the day, **same-day replacement** helps prevent ad hoc exceptions and inconsistency.

---

### 3) Unlocking Base Operations

Review whether:
- Unlocking bases are:
  - Functioning correctly
  - Located at appropriate, school-approved points
- Student flow at unlocking points remains smooth during:
  - Dismissal
  - Designated phone-use periods

Unlocking bases define **where and when** phone use is allowed; any bottlenecks or confusion should be addressed early.

---

### 4) Incident and Exception Review

Review logs or reports related to:
- Attempts to unlock pouches outside approved locations
- Missing or damaged pouches
- Repeated misunderstandings of the routine
- Emergency unlocking incidents handled by administrative staff

Patterns often indicate a **workflow or communication gap**, not student intent.

---

## Data Points to Track Over Time

Schools may find it useful to track:
- Number of pouch replacements issued per month
- Whether replacements were issued same-day
- Which levels or cohorts require more replacements
- Feedback from staff on ease of visual checks
- Feedback from students on clarity of routines

This data supports better planning for:
- Future procurement quantities
- Buffer stock sizing
- Unlocking base placement

---

## Quick Operational Improvements

Based on common observations, schools can implement low-effort refinements such as:
- Reinforcing the standard routine during form teacher briefings:
  **phone → pouch → lock → carry → unlock at base**
- Using assembly or form-time reminders to reinforce:
  - Where phones must remain locked
  - Where unlocking is allowed
- Ensuring all staff supervising CCAs, camps, or off-site activities apply the **same routine** to avoid mixed signals

Consistency across spaces is a key advantage of the programme.

---

## Escalation and Corrective Actions

If recurring issues are identified:
- Review whether students are placing excessive pressure on pouches (e.g. overfilled bags).
- Reinforce correct handling rather than introducing new controls.
- Conduct targeted briefings for specific cohorts if routines are being misunderstood.

For repeated non-compliance, schools should address the **behavioural expectation**, not redesign the workflow, keeping the programme simple and predictable.

---

## Guiding Principle

A healthy programme is one where:
- Compliance is visible at a glance
- Staff do not need to collect or handle phones
- Exceptions remain rare and clearly controlled
- The policy message stays consistent:
  **“In class, the phone is in a locked pouch.”**

Regular, light-touch operational checks ensure the programme remains effective without increasing staff workload.
