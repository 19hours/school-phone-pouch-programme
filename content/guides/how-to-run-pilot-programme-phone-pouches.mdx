## Purpose of a Pilot Programme

Running a pilot allows a Singapore school to test the **SG School Phone Pouch Programme** in a controlled setting before scaling schoolwide. The pilot focuses on validating daily routines, staff confidence, student compliance, and operational flow—rather than testing the product itself.

The objective is to confirm that the core routine works smoothly:
**phone → pouch → lock → carry → unlock at base**.

---

## Step 1: Select the Pilot Cohort

- Choose **one level or year group** with a manageable number of students.
- Ensure the cohort represents typical school routines (e.g. assemblies, classroom movement).
- Confirm leadership support so expectations are clear and consistent.

The pilot cohort should follow the same rules intended for full rollout to avoid creating special-case behaviour.

---

## Step 2: Appoint Teacher Champions

Identify a small group of staff to anchor the pilot, typically including:
- Form teachers from the selected level
- A year head or discipline lead
- A member of the school leadership team

Teacher champions help:
- Model consistent enforcement
- Provide early feedback on workflow issues
- Reinforce the simple policy message:
  **“In class, the phone is in a locked pouch.”**

---

## Step 3: Issue Pouches and Practise the Routine

For the pilot cohort:
- Issue each student a **personal phone pouch**.
- Practise:
  - Locking phones on entry to phone-free spaces
  - Carrying the locked pouch throughout the day
  - Unlocking only at designated unlocking bases when allowed

Teachers should focus on **visual compliance checks** (everyone has a locked pouch), not phone handling or spot checks.

---

## Step 4: Configure Unlocking Base Locations

- Install unlocking bases at clearly defined locations appropriate for the pilot cohort (e.g. outside classrooms or near common exits).
- Unlocking bases define **where and when** phone use is permitted.
- Ensure students understand that unlocking does not occur inside phone-free learning spaces.

Administrative staff should be briefed on emergency unlocking procedures during the pilot.

---

## Step 5: Maintain Buffer Stock and Operational Support

During the pilot period, schools should:
- Hold a **small buffer of spare pouches** to handle damage or replacements.
- Monitor wear-and-tear under normal student use.
- Track common operational questions from staff and students.

Because the pouch remains with the student all day, early clarity reduces disputes and ad hoc exceptions.

---

## Step 6: Gather Feedback and Refine

At the end of the pilot (typically one to two weeks):
- Gather feedback from:
  - Teachers (ease of checks, lesson flow)
  - Students (clarity of routine, usability)
- Review:
  - Disruption levels during lessons
  - Bottlenecks at unlocking points
  - Effectiveness of staff briefings

Adjustments may include:
- Unlocking base placement
- Staff briefing emphasis
- Parent communication clarity

---

## Step 7: Prepare for Scale-Up

Use pilot findings to:
- Finalise routines and messaging
- Confirm pouch and unlocking base quantities for the full school
- Align exception handling and emergency procedures
- Brief the wider staff body with confidence, supported by real pilot experience

A well-run pilot ensures that when the programme scales, it does so with **predictability, consistency, and minimal additional staff workload**.
