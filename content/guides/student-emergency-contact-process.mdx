## Purpose of This Process

The SG School Phone Pouch Programme is designed so that phones are **secured during lessons but remain accessible in emergencies**. This process clarifies how schools in Singapore can manage urgent situations without undermining the phone-free classroom routine.

Core principle:
**Phones remain with students, but access is controlled and supervised.**

---

## Standard Emergency Contact Approach

### How Parents Should Contact the School
- Parents should continue to contact the school through **existing channels**, typically the **general office**, for urgent matters.
- This reduces expectations that students must be reachable directly on their personal phones during curriculum hours.
- Parent communications should consistently reinforce this approach.

---

## Emergency Unlocking Procedure

### Who Can Unlock a Phone
- **Administrative staff** (e.g. general office staff or designated duty staff) are authorised to unlock phone pouches.
- Unlocking is done using a **magnetic unlocking base**.

### How Unlocking Works
- The student is directed to a **school-approved location** (e.g. general office).
- The phone pouch is unlocked using the unlocking base.
- The student accesses their phone **only for the duration required** to address the emergency.

Unlocking does **not** take place inside classrooms or other phone-free learning spaces.

---

## After Emergency Use

- Once the urgent matter has been addressed:
  - The phone is placed back into the student’s pouch.
  - The pouch is re-locked using the built-in locking clip.
- The student then returns to the lesson or activity, maintaining consistency with the normal routine.

No additional tools, seals, or numbering systems are required for this process.

---

## Staff Readiness and Visibility

Schools should ensure that:
- Staff know **where unlocking bases are located**.
- Administrative teams are briefed on emergency unlocking responsibilities.
- Teachers understand that emergencies are handled through admin support, not ad hoc unlocking in class.

This keeps the classroom environment calm and predictable, even during urgent situations.

---

## Communication to Parents

Parent-facing messages should clearly state that:
- Phones are not confiscated, but secured during lessons.
- Emergency access remains available through the school.
- The general office remains the primary contact point during the school day.

A simple reassurance statement can be used:
> “Your child’s phone is kept with them, but locked during lessons. In emergencies, the school can unlock access immediately.”

---

## Key Takeaway

A clear emergency contact process ensures that:
- Learning spaces remain phone-free
- Staff respond quickly and calmly to urgent situations
- Parents retain confidence that communication is always possible when it matters

This balance is central to the success of the SG School Phone Pouch Programme.
