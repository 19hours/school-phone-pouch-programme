## Core Design That Reduces Staffing Load

The SG School Phone Pouch Programme is designed to minimise staff workload by making compliance **visible and easy to audit**:

- Each student uses a **personal phone pouch**.
- Students lock phones into pouches when entering **phone-free spaces** (e.g. classrooms, halls, exam venues).
- The pouch uses a **child-safe locking clip** (no needles or sharp pins).
- Phones are unlocked only using a **magnetic unlocking base** at designated locations and approved times.
- The pouch stays with the student throughout the day, enabling quick checks without collecting devices.

This reduces the need for additional duty teachers because staff are not required to physically handle phones or run collection points.

---

## Practical Operating Model With Limited Staff

### 1) Standardise Routines So Teachers Don’t Need to “Manage” Phones
A low-manpower programme depends on consistent routines that students can execute independently:

- Entry: phone into pouch → lock
- During lessons: pouch remains locked
- When allowed: unlock only at approved unlocking bases

When the routine is consistent, enforcement becomes a quick visual confirmation rather than a recurring intervention.

---

### 2) Use Fixed Unlocking Bases (Not Handheld Unlockers)
The programme operates using **unlocking bases** that define where and when phones may be used.

Procurement and scaling reference:
- **1 free unlocking base for every 100 pouches purchased**
- Additional unlocking base: **$80 each**

Schools can reduce manpower needs by:
- Locating unlocking bases at predictable, high-flow points (e.g. near exits)
- Using multiple bases where needed to prevent congestion
- Avoiding ad hoc unlocking that creates crowding and negotiation

---

### 3) Optimise Student Flow With Clear Signage and Predictable Locations
With limited staff, signage and layout do most of the work:

- Clearly mark:
  - phone-free spaces
  - where unlocking is allowed
  - expected student movement during dismissal
- Ensure students understand that unlocking happens only at the base and only at approved times
- Keep the policy message simple and consistent:
  **“In class, the phone is in a locked pouch.”**

---

### 4) Leverage Student Leadership for Queue Guidance (Where Appropriate)
To keep staff workload light, schools may assign student leaders (e.g. prefects/student leaders) to:
- Direct peers to the correct unlocking points
- Encourage orderly movement at dismissal
- Reinforce routine expectations (without handling phones or unlocking)

This supports flow management without requiring additional duty teachers.

---

## What to Avoid (Creates Manpower Burden)

To keep staffing minimal, avoid introducing processes that the programme is specifically designed to eliminate, such as:
- Collecting phones in baskets or central drop-off points
- Running manual checks of devices in bags or pockets
- Ad hoc unlocking on request (which increases negotiation and crowding)

The programme’s advantage is that it enables **fast visual compliance checks** instead.

---

## Key Takeaway

A low-staff model works when the school relies on what the programme is built for:
- Visible compliance (everyone has a locked pouch)
- Simple routines students can execute independently
- Controlled access through fixed unlocking bases
- Consistency across classrooms, halls, CCAs, camps, and exams

When these elements are in place, the programme can be sustained with limited duty staffing and minimal operational friction.
