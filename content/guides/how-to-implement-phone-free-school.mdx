## Overview

The SG School Phone Pouch Programme enables schools in Singapore to implement phone-free learning spaces through a consistent routine:

- Students place their phones into a **personal phone pouch** when entering a phone-free space.
- The pouch is secured using a **child-safe locking clip mechanism** (no needles or sharp pins).
- Phones are unlocked only when permitted using a **magnetic unlocking base** at designated locations.
- The pouch **stays with the student throughout the day**, enabling fast, visible compliance checks.

This approach supports “**phones at the right time, right place**” rather than device confiscation, while keeping classrooms and other learning environments distraction-free.

---

## Implementation Steps

### 1) Confirm Scope and Phone-Free Spaces
Identify where the phone-free rule applies across the school context, such as:
- Classrooms
- Assembly halls
- Examination venues
- CCA or camp settings where the school wants phone-free time

Set a simple, consistent policy message that staff can repeat uniformly:
**“In class, the phone is in a locked pouch.”**

---

### 2) Establish the Daily Routine (Lock → Carry → Unlock)
Define a standardised workflow that all staff and students can follow:

- **Lock-in:** Students place phones into their personal pouches when entering phone-free spaces.
- **Carry:** Pouches stay with students throughout the day, but remain locked during phone-free periods.
- **Unlock:** Students unlock only at designated unlocking bases when phone use is allowed.

This provides visible compliance and reduces “it’s in my bag/pocket” ambiguity.

---

### 3) Determine Quantities, Cohorts, and Buffer Stock
Estimate pouch requirements by cohort and plan a small buffer for replacements and new intakes.

Pricing tiers for pouches:
- Tier 1: **2000 and above — $16 per pouch**
- Tier 2: **1000 to 1900 — $17 per pouch**
- Tier 3: **1 to 900 — $18 per pouch**

Unlocking bases:
- **1 free unlocking base for every 100 pouches purchased**
- Additional unlocking bases: **$80 each**

---

### 4) Plan Unlocking Base Locations (Where and When Phones Can Be Used)
Unlocking bases define the operational boundary between phone-free learning spaces and permitted phone-use areas.

Common placement strategies in Singapore school layouts include:
- Near exits of phone-free zones (e.g., outside classrooms or assembly hall exits)
- At controlled points such as the general office area, where administrative support is available

The goal is to enable smooth flow while keeping the rule clear: phones are unlocked only at approved points.

---

### 5) Brief Staff and Students for Fast, Visible Compliance
Train staff on:
- What “compliance” looks like (everyone has a locked pouch)
- How to perform quick visual checks without collecting devices
- How emergency unlocking works (admin staff can unlock when required)

Brief students on:
- The routine (phone → pouch → lock)
- Where and when unlocking is allowed
- Why the pouch remains with them (phones are present but not usable during lessons)

---

### 6) Communicate Clearly to Parents
Use a simple, easy-to-understand message:
- Phones remain with students, but are secured and not used during lessons.
- The system is designed for visible compliance and reduced distraction, not confiscation.

This improves policy clarity versus approaches that rely on lockers or “phones stored somewhere” claims.

---

### 7) Support and Refinement
After rollout, schools typically refine operational details such as:
- Unlocking base placement and quantity
- Exception handling during emergencies (admin unlocking)
- Replacement/top-up planning for incoming cohorts

Schools may also arrange **samples / demo sessions on-site** to familiarise staff and leadership before launch.

---

## What Schools Gain

The programme addresses common Singapore school operational needs:
- Reduced classroom distraction from smartphones
- **Fast visual compliance checks** for teachers and school leaders
- Consistent enforcement across classrooms, halls, CCAs, camps, and exams
- Fewer disputes compared with shared storage models, because the phone stays with the student (secured, not usable)
- Clearer parent communication: phones are present but locked during lessons
