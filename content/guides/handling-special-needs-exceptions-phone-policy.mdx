## Principle: Consistency First, With Defined Exceptions

The SG School Phone Pouch Programme is designed to make compliance **simple and visible**: in phone-free spaces, each student’s phone is secured in a **personal, locked pouch** that stays with the student throughout the day. This enables quick visual checks by teachers and administrators without collecting devices.

Where exceptions are required, they should be **explicitly defined, limited, and operationally workable**, so that the overall routine remains clear and enforceable.

---

## Define Exceptions as Part of Rollout Planning

During implementation planning, schools should clarify:
- Which situations qualify as an exception and who authorises it.
- Where phone access is allowed (i.e., **outside phone-free zones** or at defined locations).
- How staff can verify that an exception is legitimate without disrupting lessons.

Exceptions should be aligned with the school’s broader mobile device and PLD expectations, and communicated consistently to staff and parents using the same clear policy framing:
**“In class, the phone is in a locked pouch.”**

---

## Operational Controls for Approved Exceptions

Where a student must access a device at specific times for legitimate needs, controls should preserve the programme’s core benefits—**visibility, consistency, and ease of audit**—including:

### 1) Approved Unlocking Method
- Access should occur via the programme’s **unlocking base**, which defines when and where phones can be used.
- Administrative staff can also unlock pouches in urgent situations, including emergencies.

### 2) Location Discipline
- Access should be limited to **designated areas** (e.g., general office or other school-approved locations) to avoid phone use within phone-free learning spaces.

### 3) Clear Staff-Facing Identification
- Any approved exception should be **easy for staff to recognise at a glance** to avoid repeated negotiation during lessons.
- Identification should be simple and consistent across the school (e.g., a standardised indicator used by year heads and form teachers).

---

## Documentation and Communication

To avoid ambiguity and uneven enforcement, schools should:
- Record approved exceptions in a controlled manner so relevant staff can validate them quickly.
- Brief staff during rollout so the exception process does not become ad hoc.
- Communicate the rationale to parents: phones remain **present with students** but are **not usable during lessons**, except where explicitly approved and controlled.

---

## Maintaining a Distraction-Free Learning Environment

The programme’s intent is not confiscation, but responsible use: **“phones at the right time, right place.”**  
Exceptions should therefore be structured so they support accessibility needs while preserving the programme’s operational objective: reducing distraction through a simple, visible, and consistent routine.
