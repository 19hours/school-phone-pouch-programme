## Overview

The SG School Phone Pouch Programme relies on **simple, repeatable routines** at entry and exit points to avoid congestion, reduce negotiation, and ensure consistent compliance across all classes and school spaces.

Phones are never collected or stored centrally. Instead, each student keeps their phone secured in a **personal, locked pouch** that remains with them throughout the day.

---

## Morning Entry: Lock-In Routine

### When Locking Occurs
- Lock-in happens when students **enter a phone-free space**, such as:
  - Classrooms
  - Assembly halls
  - Examination venues
- This routine becomes part of the **normal start-of-lesson or start-of-day procedure**.

### How Locking Works
- Students place their phones into their **clearly labelled personal pouches** on entry.
- The pouch is secured using the **built-in child-safe locking clip**.
- No keys are used; locking is immediate and self-managed by students.
- Teachers conduct a **quick visual check** rather than handling phones directly.

### Operational Benefits
- Eliminates discreet phone use in pockets or bags.
- Provides visible, easy-to-audit compliance for teachers and administrators.
- Reduces disputes by standardising expectations across all classes.

---

## During the School Day

- While students are in phone-free spaces, the pouch:
  - Remains locked
  - Stays with the student at all times
- Phones are physically present but inaccessible, reducing distraction and under-the-table use.
- In emergencies, **administrative staff** may unlock pouches using an unlocking base.

---

## Exit and Dismissal: Unlocking Routine

### Where Unlocking Occurs
- Phones are unlocked **only at designated unlocking bases**, installed at locations chosen by the school, such as:
  - Near classroom exits
  - Assembly hall exits
  - School foyers or general office areas
- Unlocking bases define **where and when** phone use is allowed.

### How Unlocking Works
- When students are permitted to use their phones (e.g. dismissal or designated times):
  - They step outside the phone-free zone.
  - They tap their pouch on an unlocking base.
- This ensures a **clear physical separation** between learning spaces and phone-use zones.

### Preventing Bottlenecks
- Schools typically install **multiple unlocking bases** to distribute flow.
- The same unlocking routine applies across all classes, reducing confusion and negotiation.
- One unlocking base is provided free for every **100 pouches purchased**, with additional bases available if required.

---

## Handling Exceptions and Edge Cases

- Exceptions (e.g. medical or learning-related needs) should be:
  - Clearly defined during rollout
  - Aligned with existing mobile phone and PLD policies
- Schools are advised to:
  - Plan buffer quantities of pouches for replacements and new intakes
  - Locate administrative unlocking access (e.g. general office) for emergency situations

---

## Key Principles for Smooth Operations

- Keep routines **simple and consistent** across the entire school.
- Use unlocking bases—not ad hoc permissions—to manage phone access.
- Reinforce a single, clear message to students and parents:  
  **“In class, the phone is in a locked pouch.”**

When embedded into daily practice, the entry and exit workflow becomes automatic, scalable, and easy to enforce without increasing staff workload.
