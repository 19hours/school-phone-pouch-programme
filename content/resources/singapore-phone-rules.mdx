## Overview

Schools in Singapore operate under MOE’s clear expectation that **mobile devices should not disrupt learning**. While individual schools determine their detailed rules, the consistent direction is that phones should be **inaccessible during curriculum time**, with emergency contact maintained through formal school channels.

The SG School Phone Pouch Programme aligns with this direction by enabling phones to:
- Remain **with students**
- Be **physically inaccessible during lessons and other phone-free periods**
- Be accessed only in **controlled, school-approved situations**

This approach avoids confiscation while supporting focused learning and predictable school routines.

---

## MOE-Aligned Principles Reflected in School Practice

Across Singapore schools, phone rules typically reflect these shared principles:

- **Learning-first priority:** Phones should not interrupt teaching, assessments, or structured activities.
- **Clear routines:** Students and staff should know exactly what to do, without case-by-case negotiation.
- **Emergency readiness:** Parents reach students through the general office during the day; schools retain the ability to facilitate access when needed.
- **Consistency:** Relief teachers and visitors should see the same routine applied everywhere.

---

## How the Phone Pouch Workflow Fits

The SG School Phone Pouch Programme operationalises these principles through a simple, visible workflow:

- Students place their phone into a **personal lockable pouch** when entering phone-free spaces (e.g. classrooms, assembly halls, examination venues).
- The pouch uses a **child-safe locking clip** and **does not use needles or sharp pins**.
- The pouch **stays with the student throughout the day**, making compliance easy to verify visually.
- Phones are unlocked only at **school-designated unlocking bases**, at approved times.
- **Administrative staff can unlock pouches during emergencies** using an unlocking base.

The core message remains consistent:
**“In class, the phone is in a locked pouch.”**

---

## What This Means for Schools (Operationally)

### Entry Workflow
- Phone locking becomes part of the normal start-of-lesson or start-of-day routine.
- Students secure phones in their own pouches; phones are **not handed to teachers** and are **not collected centrally**.
- Teachers conduct quick visual checks instead of handling devices.

### Storage vs Carrying the Pouch
- In classrooms and assemblies, the locked pouch stays with the student.
- During examinations, the locked pouch is typically **kept inside the student’s bag**.
- For CCAs, camps, and off-site activities, the same rule applies: phone locked, pouch with the student or in their bag, depending on activity logistics.

This flexibility allows schools to meet different operational needs without changing the core routine.

---

### Enforcement Consistency
- Enforcement is based on **visible compliance**, not device searches.
- Teachers verify that students have a locked pouch in phone-free spaces.
- This avoids disputes such as “my phone is in my bag” and reduces reliance on confiscation.

The programme intentionally does **not** rely on seals, serial checks, or termly refresh cycles unless a school independently chooses to add such controls.

---

### Administrative Effort
- Schools maintain a **buffer stock of spare pouches** for loss or damage.
- Replacement procedures are straightforward and centrally managed (e.g. via the general office).
- Procurement documentation references the local supplier:

This supports continuity of operations without supply-related disruption.
